Q.6.2  

You will see that there are overall generic descriptions for the three levels of response at the top of the scheme, giving a hierarchy 
of response. Within each Level of Response there are two marks. The indicative points in the 
mark scheme are divided into headings relating to Type 1, Type 2 and the importance of the test. 
When marking this (and other extended response questions) you must NOT use the tick 
annotation (as if a school / student requests to see the paper the annotations will remain and 
they can justifiably expect a tick to represent a mark). Annotations are not required but using 
them will help you arrive at a mark. We ask that you use the star annotation, along with highlight 
or underlining tools if you wish. Please do NOT use the eye symbol as this has consequences 
for reviews. 

This is not marked as 1 idea gains 1 mark, 5 ideas gain 5 marks etc. 
You may find it helpful to number the bullet points and make a note of which number bp you see 
(on a sheet of paper, not on screen) to help you decide the level and then the mark (and to avoid 
crediting the same idea several times). 

Please familiarise yourselves with the question to ensure credit is not being given where 
candidates are simply repeating detail that is in the stem.  Credit should not be awarded for 
incorrect linking between C-peptide and insulin injections. 
For BP1, in Type 1 diabetes, it would be insufficient to simply state ‘the person’ does not produce 
insulin. The pancreas needs to be included. 

For BP2, no C-peptide would be equivalent to low C-peptide. 
BP3 cannot be awarded if BP2 is not credited. The two BPs are linked with the words ‘even if.’ 
In Type 2, BP4, equivalent words for the body / cells could be muscle / liver.  It would be 
insufficient to say that in Type 2 the body is ‘immune to insulin’, ‘can’t detect insulin’, ‘rejects 
insulin’ or that ‘insulin doesn’t work’, but becoming ‘resistant to insulin’, ‘insensitive to insulin’ or 
‘not reacting to insulin’ could gain credit. 

For BP5, ‘People with Type 2 have increased insulin’ or ‘Type 2 have insulin’ would be 
equivalent to insulin being produced. 

In BP6, it would be insufficient to simply state that C-peptide would be made or that its 
concentration would fluctuate. 

However, stating it would be a normal level would be equivalent. 
For BP7, in the ‘importance’ indicative content ‘insulin shots’ is taken as being equivalent to 
‘insulin injections’.  The idea that ‘in Type 2, insulin injections will not work’ is insufficient. 
BP8 – stating that insulin is dangerous, would be too high, would cause a diabetic coma or death 
would not be credited. 

BP10 – statements such as healthy diet / lifestyle would be insufficient for this BP. Candidates 
can give the converse such as ‘Type 2 is more likely if you are inactive / obese’.  ‘Type 2 is 
caused by a lack of exercise’ or ‘people have Type 2 because of a bad diet’ would be 
insufficient.
 
To enter level 3   
Note the statement at the end of the indicative content.   
For Level 3, details of Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes must be considered, with the link to  
C-peptide and the importance of the test described.  
So for level 3, there must be: 
Indicative content from each of the 3 headings 
and 
Indicative content relating to C-peptide (this will be BP2 and / or BP6) 
and 
At least one logical link within one of the 3 headings. 

To enter level 2 
• linked to  •  
The logical link can be between any bullet points within one of the 3 headings on the mark 
scheme.   
The link must show some logic.  So, ‘People with Type 2 could stop having insulin injections, 
which have side effects (BP7 and BP8) is a logical link. 
However, there is no linking between some BPs.  e.g. insulin injections have side effects and 
are expensive (BP8 and BP9).  These are just 2 different statements with no logical link. 
If there is more than the above requirement, but insufficient to achieve level 3, consider a 
higher mark in level 2 

To enter level 1 
• any indicative content  
Anything more than this, but insufficient to achieve level 2, consider a higher mark in level 1 
For example,  
“In Type 1, the pancreas does not produce insulin” 1 mark 
“In Type 1, the C-peptide would be low because the pancreas does not produce insulin” 3 marks 
“In Type 1, the C-peptide would be low because the pancreas does not produce insulin, but in 
Type 2 the C-peptide would be high” 4 marks 
“In Type 1, the pancreas does not produce insulin.  In Type 2, the body cells no longer respond 
to insulin but insulin is still produced.  Type 2 diabetes can be treated with a carbohydrate
controlled diet.”  4 marks The student has given detail from the 3 headings, and a logical link is 
given, but they have not linked this to C-peptide, therefore cannot enter level 3. 

“In Type 1, the C-peptide would be low because the pancreas does not produce insulin, but in 
Type 2 the C-peptide would be high.  If they have Type 2, they could stop insulin injections.” 5
