Q. 05.6  
      
This is an Extended Response question. Please read the information in the introduction to 
this commentary document regarding how to annotate students' responses.
 
You may find it helpful to download the infographic (star-chart) from the additional supporting 
information in OLS. This shows what details are required to gain access (i.e. achieve the 
lower mark) for each Level in a visual format 

To enter Level 3 (5 marks), the student must have a linked statement between a hormone, 
its role and its consequence for BOTH contraception and fertility.  

To enter Level 2 (3 marks), there are several ways the student can do this. They can have a 
full linked statement including hormone, role and consequence for EITHER contraception 
or infertility.  
  
Alternatively, they can partially link the hormone/role/consequence for BOTH contraception 
and fertility.  This can be mixed and matched e.g. A hormone and role for contraception AND 
a hormone and consequence for fertility.  

To enter Level 1 (1 mark), We need reference to any relevant content, but note there is a 
maximum of 1 mark for contraceptive method(s) only.  We are ignoring references to non
hormonal methods of contraception. 

Any additional content, beyond the minimum required for entry to the level, (but short of the 
level above it) will increase the mark to the higher mark in the level (2, 4 or 6).  For example, 
the reference to a contraceptive method would enable the student to access the higher mark 
within a Level. 

If students mention relevant hormones and then give their roles and/or consequences in the 
correct respective order, we can give this credit only if they write ‘respectively’.  
E.g. An answer such as ‘Progesterone inhibits the release of FSH and LH which prevent the 
maturation and ovulation respectively.’  - consequences are clearly linked to hormones.
 
Whereas ‘Progesterone inhibits the release of FSH and LH which prevent the maturation 
and ovulation’ would not be sufficient for a clearly linked consequence. 
In the contraception section we cannot give credit for saying what FSH and LH normally do, 
the indicative content focuses on the consequence of a lack of these hormones. 
Preventing egg growth is too vague in terms of egg development/maturation. 
Other uses of hormones in contraception are creditworthy e.g. ‘Progesterone thickens 
cervical mucus which hinders/prevents passage of sperm.’
