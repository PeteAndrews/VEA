Q03.2 

This is an ‘Extended Response’ style of question. Such questions are marked holistically. You will see that there are overall generic descriptors for the two levels of response at the top of the scheme, giving a hierarchy of response. Within each Level of Response there are two marks. The indicative content in the mark scheme is divided into three blocks. 

NOTE that the mark scheme shows indicative content. Hence, the list of bullet points is not exhaustive, so use your professional judgement as appropriate if the student suggests ideas that are not given in the mark scheme. The question is set at high demand, so any points you consider might be creditworthy must be matched to this demand. To gain credit at Level 2, the student must refer to details of both the primary and secondary responses in animals, along with how this prevents infection of humans. For Level 2 the idea of ‘new’ antibodies are made in the animal not that they simply hanging around from the primary response, is required. To gain credit in Level 1 descriptions of both primary and secondary responses in animals are required. At this level we would expect students to refer to white blood cells, rather than ‘WBC’. Any mention of the full term in any part of the response will suffice if they give this abbreviation. If there is no full term in any part of the response, then ‘WBC’ is acceptable on second and subsequent occasions. Of course, students might alternatively refer to ‘lymphocytes’. 

If the term ‘virus’ is used throughout, instead of bacteria, then the answer can only achieve Level 1.
