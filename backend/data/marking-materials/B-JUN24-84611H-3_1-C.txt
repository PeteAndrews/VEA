To achieve a mark in Level 3, students must give details of both reagents and both positive results, as well as at least one risk with its linked control measure. In addition, students must include good scientific terminology – as such, we would expect Level 3 students to be clear in their wording, e.g. iodine solution (which could be inferred by using ‘drops’) in the starch test, and in the Benedict’s test they should convey a clear understanding of the need to heat the mixture appropriately. 

For Level 2, a description of the reagent used to test for either starch or sugar should be given, as well as the positive test result for the named reagent. In addition, the student must give an additional piece of indicative content from any one of the three blocks. 

At the most basic level, Level 1, students must give an indicative content point from any block. 

When considering detail from the ‘Risk Assessment’ part of the indicative content, at Level 3, please note we are looking for an understanding of the risk, as well as giving a specific measure to control the risk. For Level 3 we need to see both a black bullet point (bp) and a related white bp for the risk assessment, whereas at Level 2 a black bp alone is sufficient. Note that a white bp alone (control measure) is not creditworthy at any level. 

Good examples of a risk assessment are: 
 • ‘Iodine solution is an irritant if it gets on the skin, so we should wear gloves.’ 
 • ‘Benedict’s solution is harmful, and we need to be careful when using it so as not to spill on ourselves and wash it off if we do.’ 
 • ‘Benedict’s solution needs to be heated, so we should wear goggles.’ 
 • ‘Benedict’s solution needs to be heated so we should be careful when using it so it doesn’t spill on us. Hot test tubes should be cooled down before clearing it away’ 
 • ‘You should wear lab gloves on wash hands immediately if you spill it on them’ 

Other answers are creditworthy if they meet general laboratory rules not specifically linked to using these reagents, and similarly vague ideas such as ‘breaking glassware’ or ‘causing stains’ are also insufficient. Remember that students may give creditworthy control measures that are not detailed on the indicative content (such as the use of ‘heat protective gloves’).
